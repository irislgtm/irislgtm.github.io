# Cancel culture and drama with Leclowndu in the MMC community

Hello. This is my sixth entry on this blog and in this entry I am going to talk about some experiences I have had with cancel culture and how it is affecting modded Minecraft in this state.

Cancel culture is a thing that has been going on for quite a while now on the internet. Originally, when we think of cancel culture, we think of things like if a rapper or musician has said something controversial on social media, they would be reprimanded for that and their content would be socially boycotted.

This can apply to public figures, in my opinion, but for more private people or small communities, this can also be quite harmful, especially when the person has said something many years ago that they now regret and have apologized for, which in this case can be seen in the Thaumaturge Minecraft drama.

So originally, before we begin with the drama itself, I would like to talk about the developer of the mod. Thaumaturge was developed by a person called Leclowndu93150, who had been in the GregTech modding community for the past few years, or at least until he was banned in 2023. Why had he gotten banned? Well, it was because he had said some extremely disturbing things to one of the members of the GregTech Community Edition developer community, which caused him to be banned. Considering his other inappropriate behavior there, it was warranted at that time, but in my opinion, it is an important thing to consider that this was in 2023. Taking his current age into account, which is 19 or 18, from what I have seen on his website, 3 years ago he would have been 16 or 15, and that is an age where people make a lot of stupid decisions and say a lot of stupid things in general.

So I think that rather than reprimanding him for this mistake for the entirety of his career, we should instead focus on the fact that he has apologized. I do know that you have to apologize to someone and have them accept your apology, and it may be possible that he is being disingenuous, but I believe that he has done all he can without making the situation worse for himself, and that he may actually regret what he has said in the past, and it is probably time to let that go. It is quite unfortunate to see that someone like him is still reprimanded for his actions 3 years after the fact.

In my opinion, no matter the words he said, he did not commit an actual crime. I mean, it could be classified as hate speech in a way, but again, he was a minor at that time, and we all have said some stupid stuff in the past ourselves. So, that is basically the drama surrounding him. I'd say it is overblown to this date. I was participating in it too, and I have done quite a bit of research. Most of the people who I have asked have generally said that he has improved over the past few years, and he has both apologized and reformed. So, I don't think it is prudent to drag this drama out onto him much further. Plus, I also doubt that the GregTech Community Edition developers have a very clean past themselves, from what I've heard, although that conversation is for another time. Other than that, I'd like to talk about the main copyright-related drama that has been going on. So, the gist of it is that once Thaumcraft 6 was done in 2018, the main developer of Thaumcraft left development permanently. He hadn't responded to anyone in a while, but he had given permission to CoFH, which is a popular modding group that has existed for quite a while now. They have made a lot of old mods like CoFH Core and mods like Thermal Expansion, which was popular in 1.7.10 and 1.12.2.

However, he only gave them permission to develop Thaumcraft for modern Minecraft in the form of Thaumcraft 7. He did not give them full rights over Thaumcraft, and Azanor, who is the original developer of the mod, still retains the license and copyright over the mod. It was just a simple permission that he had given to them to allow them to port the mod to new versions. So they have been working on this for a few years now, but due to their own difficulties and managing their other mods, they have been unable to develop it for quite a while. Considering that he left in 2018, it has been almost 8 years, I'd say, since Azanor disappeared, and it has been quite a long time since we have seen any progress on Thaumcraft 7.

From CoFH, we have seen a lot of screenshots and teaser trailers with custom art and possibly new mechanics being made for the new Thaumcraft version. But there was also a document from CoFH which detailed their development process. So, they had detailed in this doc how they were unable to develop Thaumcraft 7 for quite a while and why they were not hiring any more people for their team. Apparently, there have been multiple incidents in which people who volunteered to make Thaumcraft 7 hadn't contributed to the mod at all and that disheartened the CoFH team. Other than that, there was also an incident a couple of years ago where the developer of Malum: Voidbound was alleged to have stolen Thaumcraft 7 concepts and code and used them in his own mod while pretending that he would help with Thaumcraft 7 development. This was a couple of years ago though, and CoFH had also admitted back then that they had preemptively filed a takedown request on Malum: Voidbound without having conclusive evidence of wrongdoing. This shows a pattern of CoFH being sensitive towards these things. So, I would say that this pattern definitely applies to the modern Thaumcraft 7 issues which we will get to now. So, the guy I have been talking about is called Leclowndu and had recently made a Thaumcraft port to modern Minecraft called Thaumaturge. This mod was less of a proper remake of Thaumcraft than Thaumcraft 7 was intended to be, but rather a port of the mod to modern Minecraft. It ported content from Thaumcraft 4 and 6, and Thaumcraft 4 was considered the better version of the mod. And there was probably AI used in the porting of the mod, which I wouldn't say is a big deal because most large modders use AI nowadays, and it only really matters when the content is visibly low effort. This topic is a wildfire in the modding community right now though, and it has many different angles and people arguing for many different sides. So, I would say it is not that easy to judge a mod just based on whether it used AI or not. There is also how much effort was put into it by the developer himself, how they developed, what assets were used, and how much experience the developer has.

However, I would say that this is quite a high-quality port compared to low-effort AI ports of this mod, and it is generally one of the better ones as it also planned to adapt the mod properly to modern Minecraft instead of doing sloppy porting with AI directly doing everything for the mod. It is one of the better uses of AI in modding, and the developer himself has also been a modder for a decent amount of time before AI existed. About AI, I have used AI in the past for mod development. I would say that it had been a bad habit for me in the past, around early 2026, but I had grown out of it slowly over time, and now I don't use it as much.

In early 2025, I had been developing mods on my own using Java directly, and I had begun using AI and started relying on it heavily during the time I had made my GregTech fork, which is probably visible in the fork's quality as well. I was struggling with the fees for the AI, and I was also struggling with ethical questions about whether it was right to use it or not. The one-month break I took after developing that GregTech fork was essential for disconnecting from AI and helping myself learn those old skills again, and now I am back to being a decently advanced developer of modded Minecraft without using AI all that much. Although I will say that I do use local models every now and again to do repetitive tasks or do security checks that I don't want to do myself. It is quite useful in that aspect, but I would say that it is not good to rely on it completely, and it shows in the effort and the quality of the mod itself if you use it wrongly. But anyways, what I mean to say is that Thaumaturge is not a low-effort slop port, and it certainly is one of the better ports I've seen across these years.

After that, we have to get to the main reason CoFH is targeting Thaumaturge, which is that it was mostly copied over from Thaumcraft 4 and 6. The thing is that Thaumaturge had a lot of assets and code that were directly ported from Thaumcraft 4 and 6. I think it was mostly 6, but anyways, Thaumcraft had a lot of matching assets, although most of these were third-party or MIT-licensed assets that matched between Thaumcraft and Thaumaturge, and there has been a lot of code that was reused between the versions. It is not a proper remake in the sense that Thaumcraft 7 was intended to be, but it was decently good for being a port using AI. However, the recent noise about this port has caused CoFH to take advantage of this and, according to the developer, try to convince the big modpack teams, that is ATM and FTB, to not use the mod at all in their modpacks. And as they control a lot of modded Minecraft, it is quite concerning that they have such a monopoly over these mods. What I am about to say next is from a conversation I had with the developer himself. And that was regarding some statements he made saying that the ATM and FTB team had told him to give up development on the mod, because in their eyes he was too controversial and unstable to continue developing the mod, and that if he did not follow their directions, they would kill the mod, or not use it in their packs, not letting it get off the ground in general. Now, I think that is a pretty scummy move if that is actually true. If it is not true, then I would imagine that the mod itself would be quite successful on its own, like a couple I have seen in the past that are not included in the big packs. The big packs do not include Malum for the most part. And if the mod itself has enough quality, then why wouldn't they? They have also been entrenched in transphobic issues themselves regarding ATM, so why would they be concerned about such a thing? It is quite odd to say the least. Other than that, CoFH is acting quite scummy about it in general, because they don't have the original rights to do any copyright strikes on it. They only received permission to develop Thaumcraft 7, and they don't have the original rights over it. It is reasonable to assume that they have the ability to publish it on the platforms, but a direct copyright strike from them is not feasible in my eyes. I am sure that if they are reading this, they will have something to say. I would like them to release the exact list of infringements that have been made, but from my time interviewing the developer about the Thaumaturge mod, I doubt that is the case. In general, I hope that this mod gets released, as I feel bad for the developer and how he has been treated. I hope that FTB and ATM try to be more rational about this, and in general I hope for this mod to be successful. Thank you for reading, and goodbye.
